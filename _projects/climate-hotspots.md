## Question

Where will temperature and precipitation extremes intensify most under future climate scenarios, and what does that mean for regions and sectors such as coastal tourism?

## Approach

Analysis of CMIP6 climate model simulations and other large spatiotemporal datasets to identify hotspots of change in temperature and precipitation extremes, regionally (Turkey) and globally (MSc thesis), and to assess coastal climate impacts on human comfort.

## What I found

Globally, once the change in 28 seasonal climate indicators was weighted by population, the strongest hotspots for 2026–2050 under SSP2-4.5 lay in densely populated regions such as China, Japan and Indonesia. The pattern for 2076–2099 largely repeated the near-future one, and in the near term SSP2-4.5 and SSP3-7.0 differed less than expected.

For Turkey, a 10 km regional projection marked Southeastern Anatolia, Eastern Anatolia and the Mediterranean as hotspots under RCP4.5, with Central Anatolia added under RCP8.5; temperature-related indicators weighed most. At the ten main coastal tourism destinations, the Discomfort Index showed a statistically significant upward trend under both scenarios, pointing to more days of heat discomfort in 2026–2050 and 2051–2075.
